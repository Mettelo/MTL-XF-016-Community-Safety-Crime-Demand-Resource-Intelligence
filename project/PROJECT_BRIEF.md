# Project Brief

## Client Commission
The partnership has limited neighbourhood and prevention resources, but current reporting does not distinguish persistent demand, emerging crime categories, force-area variation and changes in recorded-crime patterns well enough to support targeted intervention.

> **Decision to support:** Which crime-demand patterns and areas should receive the earliest multi-agency investigation or prevention attention, and what evidence should be monitored before resources are reallocated?

## Primary Dataset
**Home Office Police Recorded Crime Open Data — Police Force Area tables, year ending March 2013 onwards**  
Publisher: Home Office  
Official source: https://www.gov.uk/government/statistical-data-sets/police-recorded-crime-and-outcomes-open-data-tables

## Delivery Lifecycle
**Discovery → Requirements → Governance → Acquisition → Engineering → Data Quality → Analysis → Decision Framework → BI/Product → Assurance/UAT → Executive Recommendation → Handover**

## Cross-Functional Roles
| Role | Expected contribution |
|---|---|
| Business Analyst | Define prevention/resource decisions, stakeholder needs and acceptance criteria. |
| Data Governance Analyst | Govern offence definitions, geographic reference data, lineage and quality. |
| Data Engineer | Prepare reproducible extraction/transformation from Home Office tables. |
| Community Safety/Data Analyst | Analyse demand, offence mix, trends and persistent variation. |
| BI Developer | Create operational and executive community-safety decision support. |
| GIS/Spatial Analyst | Provide proportionate geographic interpretation where appropriate. |
| Risk/Assurance Analyst | Challenge interpretation, thresholds and unintended consequences. |

## Business Questions
1. What is the current baseline and where is material variation concentrated?
2. Which data-quality, coverage or definition issues could materially alter the decision?
3. Which segments, areas, assets, recipients or corridors require deeper investigation?
4. Which indicators are suitable for prioritisation, and which should not be used that way?
5. What intervention choices are genuinely supported by evidence?
6. What should management monitor after handover?

## Success Criteria
Traceable requirements; governed definitions/lineage/quality; reproducible transformations; defensible crime-demand trends, offence mix, force-area variation, persistence, emerging changes and resource-prioritisation indicators; transparent decision logic; useful BI/product; UAT evidence; executive recommendation; maintainable handover; visible collaborative contribution.

## Panel Challenge
The review panel may challenge assumptions, denominators, definitions, data quality, thresholds, models, spatial interpretation, governance, recommendations and maintainability.
